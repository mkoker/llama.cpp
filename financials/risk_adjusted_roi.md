# Risk-adjusted ROI methodology

Date: 2026-05-21
Mission: voice-ai-pilot-validation-pack-v1
Artifact status: methodology for pilot validation; no measured pilot outcomes yet
Related model: `financials/risk_adjusted_roi.py`

## Purpose

The base ROI analysis estimates the financial upside of voice AI pilots under Conservative, Base, and Aggressive scenarios. This methodology adds an explicit risk adjustment so the business case does not treat unvalidated pilot assumptions as already-proven value.

The adjustment discounts expected annual net benefit for four named risk categories:

1. adoption risk
2. latency risk
3. compliance risk
4. provider risk

The result is a risk-adjusted NPV, ROI percentage, annual net benefit, and payback estimate for each scenario. The model is intentionally simple: every discount is named, bounded, documented, and replaceable with measured pilot data.

## Inputs

The Python model uses the same broad planning structure as the main ROI analysis:

- Conservative scenario: 60% modeled rollout, 25% contact-center AHT reduction, higher implementation cost.
- Base scenario: 80% modeled rollout, 35% contact-center AHT reduction, mid implementation cost.
- Aggressive scenario: 100% modeled rollout, 45% contact-center AHT reduction, lower implementation cost.
- Three-year time horizon.
- 10% discount rate.
- Annual maintenance equal to 15% of implementation cost.
- Planning labor pools for contact-center agents, physicians, and fraud analysts.
- Planning per-minute voice AI costs by vertical.

These are planning assumptions. They must be replaced or recalibrated after pilot measurement.

## Risk adjustment formula

For each scenario:

```text
gross annual net benefit = labor savings - provider/API cost - annual maintenance
composite pass factor = adoption pass factor * latency pass factor * compliance pass factor * provider pass factor
risk-adjusted annual net benefit = gross annual net benefit * composite pass factor
risk-adjusted cash flow year 0 = risk-adjusted annual net benefit - one-time implementation cost
risk-adjusted cash flow years 1..N = risk-adjusted annual net benefit
risk-adjusted NPV = discounted value of risk-adjusted cash flows
risk-adjusted ROI = (risk-adjusted annual net benefit * time horizon years) / one-time implementation cost
risk-adjusted payback months = one-time implementation cost / risk-adjusted annual net benefit * 12
```

Each pass factor is bounded from 0.0 to 1.0. A pass factor of 0.90 means the modeled value keeps 90% of that category’s expected benefit and applies a 10% risk discount.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|

The Aggressive scenario has the largest risk haircut because 100% adoption and higher productivity assumptions are more fragile. The Conservative scenario already embeds lower rollout, but it still carries compliance and latency uncertainty because those blockers are not solved by smaller scale.

## Bounded discount-factor policy

Every risk factor is bounded as a pass factor from 0.0 to 1.0 before it is used in the composite risk adjustment. Values outside that range are clamped by `risk_adjusted_roi.py` so a bad input cannot create negative value, inflate value above the gross model, or silently reverse the discount.

| Factor | Allowed pass-factor range | Planning range used | Bound rationale |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Adoption | 0.00-1.00 | 0.80-0.90 | Cannot exceed eligible sustained usage; low measured active use should directly reduce modeled benefit. |
| Latency | 0.00-1.00 | 0.86-0.90 | Cannot reward slow or failed turn-taking; pass rate should reflect interactions meeting vertical p95 thresholds. |
| Compliance | 0.00-1.00 | 0.80-0.85 | Cannot override legal launch blockers; residual pass factor only applies after required approval gates are cleared. |
| Provider | 0.00-1.00 | 0.88-0.92 | Cannot exceed gross value; provider reliability, cost, and fallback failures can only preserve or reduce expected benefit. |

Default interpretation: `1.00` means no discount for that risk category, `0.00` means no modeled benefit survives that category, and the current planning factors are placeholders until pilot evidence replaces them.

## Discount rationale

### Adoption risk

Adoption risk captures the difference between eligible users and sustained active use.

Measured pilot inputs:

- weekly active users / eligible users
- opt-out rate
- manual override rate
- workflow bypass rate
- qualitative friction from physicians, agents, or analysts

Why it matters:

- Seat rollout does not equal value capture.
- Users may abandon the tool if correction burden, call friction, trust, or UI workflow cost is too high.
- Customer-facing voice flows may also suffer from customer opt-outs or refusal to engage with AI.

Current treatment:

- Conservative: 10% extra discount because the scenario already models only 60% rollout.
- Base: 15% discount because 80% active adoption still needs proof.
- Aggressive: 20% discount because 100% active adoption is unlikely without heavy workflow integration and change management.

Pilot replacement rule:

Use measured active adoption as the first-order adjustment. If 80 of 100 eligible users complete the required weekly workflow volume and opt-outs are acceptable, the adoption pass factor should be near 0.80 before any additional friction penalty.

### Latency risk

Latency risk captures the possibility that measured voice interaction speed is too slow for natural turn-taking or operational productivity.

Measured pilot inputs:

- p50, p95, and p99 end-to-end response latency
- endpointing delay
- time to first token or first audio
- provider timeout rate
- fallback activation rate
- AHT impact when tail latency is poor

Why it matters:

- A voice agent can look good in demos and still fail in live workflows if p95/p99 latency breaks the conversation.
- Contact-center savings disappear if tail latency increases average handle time.
- Healthcare and fraud workflows require low-friction turn-taking because interruptions, corrections, and confirmations are part of the job.

Current treatment:

- Conservative: 12% latency discount for unmeasured buyer-network tail behavior.
- Base: 10% discount because target thresholds are plausible but not validated.
- Aggressive: 14% discount because aggressive productivity gains are more exposed to poor turn-taking.

Pilot replacement rule:

Compute a latency pass factor from threshold compliance by vertical. A simple first version:

```text
latency pass factor = interactions within p95 threshold / total measured interactions
```

If tail latency materially increases AHT or correction work, apply the productivity impact directly to gross benefit rather than only using a percentage haircut.

### Compliance risk

Compliance risk captures unresolved legal, privacy, consent, recording, PHI, biometric, retention, auditability, and vendor-contract exposure.

Measured pilot inputs:

- BAA status for healthcare workflows involving PHI
- consent disclosure pass rate
- recording and retention approval
- biometric-processing approval where applicable
- audit-log completeness
- data residency and vendor data-processing review
- unresolved legal exceptions

Why it matters:

- Compliance is not just an expected-value risk. Some failures are launch blockers.
- A workflow can be technically successful and still be a no-go if PHI, consent, biometric, or call-recording rules are unresolved.
- Fraud and healthcare pilots need human-in-loop auditability before production-like exposure.

Current treatment:

- Conservative: 20% compliance discount because PHI, consent, recording, and biometric approvals are launch blockers until cleared.
- Base: 15% discount for unresolved legal and data-handling controls.
- Aggressive: 18% discount because broader rollout increases regulated exposure.

Pilot replacement rule:

Use two layers:

1. Launch-blocker gate: if required legal approval is missing, the pilot cannot progress to production regardless of ROI.
2. Residual compliance pass factor: if legal gates are cleared but audit exceptions remain, discount expected benefit for remediation burden and rollout limits.

Do not hide a hard compliance no-go inside a small percentage discount.

### Provider risk

Provider risk captures reliability, availability, billing variance, vendor concentration, quality drift, queueing, and integration failure.

Measured pilot inputs:

- provider error rate
- timeout rate
- fallback rate
- incident count and duration
- cost per completed interaction
- variance between expected and billed minutes
- vendor data-retention and logging posture
- failover readiness

Why it matters:

- Voice AI ROI can be erased by provider cost overruns, downtime, quality drift, or brittle orchestration.
- Managed voice-agent platforms may simplify launch but add dependency risk and observability limits.
- Unit economics must be tied to completed workflows, not attempted calls or demo minutes.

Current treatment:

- Conservative: 10% provider discount.
- Base: 8% provider discount.
- Aggressive: 12% provider discount because higher volume increases queueing, billing, and concentration exposure.

Pilot replacement rule:

Provider pass should combine reliability and cost validation. Example:

```text
provider pass factor = successful billable interactions within expected cost band / attempted interactions
```

If provider cost per completed workflow exceeds the model input, update gross annual net benefit directly before applying residual provider risk.

## How to use after pilot data exists

1. Replace planning productivity assumptions with measured deltas:
   - contact-center AHT and after-call-work reduction
   - physician documentation minutes saved net of correction time
   - fraud resolution speedup and audit reduction
2. Replace planning cost assumptions with billing exports and measured voice minutes.
3. Replace risk pass factors with measured pass factors from the measurement plan.
4. Re-run `financials/risk_adjusted_roi.py`.
5. Compare gross ROI, risk-adjusted ROI, and launch-blocker status by vertical.
6. Feed the results into `pilot_validation/go_no_go_checklist.md`.

## Decision rules

- Go: measured productivity and cost produce positive risk-adjusted ROI; latency meets vertical threshold; adoption is sustained; compliance launch blockers are cleared.
- Conditional go: ROI is positive but one risk category requires remediation with named owner and date.
- Pause: pilot data is incomplete, inconsistent, or not comparable to baseline.
- No-go: compliance blocker remains unresolved, latency degrades workflow productivity, adoption fails, provider cost/reliability breaks unit economics, or quality/correction burden erases savings.

## Caveats

- This methodology is not a substitute for legal review.
- Current numbers are planning outputs, not measured pilot outcomes.
- Vendor capability claims remain evidence for feasibility only until target-environment benchmarks, billing exports, QA samples, and compliance approvals replace them.
- Risk discounts are deliberately visible and debatable; hidden confidence haircuts are not acceptable for investment decisions.
